Families applying to both an international school and a local, private, or DSS school often build two portfolios that contradict each other: an English "research project" and a Chinese certificate book. Detail questions expose the gap. The steadier approach is one real interest, one publication, and two ways of telling it.

## Is the portfolio the same object for international and local interviews?

It can be the same physical book. It should not be two stories. International interviews more often want curiosity, how the project was done, and an explanation in English. Local, private, and DSS interviews more often look at Chinese expression, how the family supported the work, and whether the file can be remembered quickly. Each school sets its own criteria. This is a difference in how to talk about a portfolio, not an official requirement, and Kidsmybook has no school partnership.

## Should the book be in Chinese or English?

It depends on the target school and is decided in consultation: Chinese, English, or bilingual. If you are applying to both, keep the child's own observations and sentences in the book, then prepare the spoken version in the interview language. Do not publish two books whose contents do not match. Why one deep interest is easier to remember than a full activity list: https://kidsmybook.com/blog/international-school-interview-portfolio-highlight .

## Can both school types share one story?

Yes, and they should. The story is where the interest came from, what was observed or organised, which page the child decided, and what was revised. An international interview can spend more time on the project and on questions. A local or DSS interview can spend more time opening one page and on how the family finished it together. The evidence is the same ISBN publication, with professor review when that is part of the work.
