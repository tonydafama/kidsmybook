Parents who search for government and aided Primary One discretionary places often finish the thought with “prepare a thick portfolio for the interview”, or “hand in the ISBN”. This page does not start from a binder. The Kidsmybook lead is publishing plus a physical bookstore listing: the child takes part, an about-12-month mentored programme prints the book, and the title and package then follow a supply path toward a Hong Kong physical bookstore listing / distribution path. A school file can cite that result. It is a second use. It is not the first sentence of the plan, and it is not a school place.

Three scenes get mixed together. The first is Discretionary Places under the Primary One Admission system, often called POA, at government and aided primary schools. Schools there follow a set procedure and a points system. They do not take a portfolio. The second is a DSS, private, or international school running its own admission. A briefing or an interview is common. Some of those schools take a thin file, and the day may allow a physical object. The third is door-knocking after central allocation: a family asks whether a place is still open. That is not the discretionary-places round. The publishing path comes first. Which scene can actually use a formal publication comes after that.

## Do government and aided Primary One discretionary places ask for a portfolio?

No. The Education Bureau’s public reminder for this stage is that schools process applications under the established procedure and criteria, so parents do not need to prepare a portfolio for the child. The same public Primary One Admission notes say schools must not test or interview applicant children on knowledge they have already been taught. What the application asks for is identity, proof of the residential address, and the proofs that apply to the points items on the form. It is not a stack of awards, and it is not a book. The form, the dates, and the notes follow that year’s Education Bureau release.

A formal publication is not a document this stage asks for, and it does not change the order of the points. Do not photocopy the whole book into the application, and do not treat an “interview for discretionary places” as a requirement of this stage. ## At DSS, private, and international schools, which scenes actually call for a published book?

When that school takes a thin file, or the interview day allows a physical object. DSS, private, and international schools do not use the government and aided points system. The school sets its own admission. Some state a limit of two to five A4 pages. Some allow a book on the day. The page count, whether a portfolio is accepted, and whether a physical object may be carried in, follow that school’s latest guidelines. ## Is door-knocking after central allocation the same thing as discretionary places?

No. Door-knocking happens after the central-allocation result, when a family asks a school whether a place is still open. It is not the Discretionary Places stage of Primary One Admission, and it does not use that points system. Whether anyone meets the family, whether a file is accepted, and whether an object may be carried in, is that school’s choice on the day. If the day allows the book, use the same page: the title, what the child did, and optionally one line on the listing path. If the day has no meeting, or takes no extra file, do not add a binder the school did not ask for.

A government or aided discretionary-places round that does not take a portfolio does not mean every later scene has nothing to say about a book. The split is this: discretionary places do not take that file. A DSS, private, or international school, or a door-knocking visit, may have a short telling. ## Can a published book guarantee a school place?

No. Kidsmybook is a publishing studio. It is not an admissions agent, it has no school partnership, and it does not represent any government, aided, DSS, private, or international school. The school decides. A listing path also does not guarantee stock in every branch. Government and aided discretionary places follow that year’s Education Bureau notes and the points criteria. Other schools follow that school’s latest guidelines. In a scene that actually allows the telling, a book can give the conversation one publication someone can check. It does not replace the points, and it does not replace a school’s admissions criteria.

## If discretionary places or an interview are only days away, should a family open a new book in order to submit a file?

If the file is only being thought of now, the 12-month plan will not finish for this round, and a physical listing path will not appear in those days either. The discretionary-places stage does not take a portfolio, so there is nothing to rush-print for that round. If a DSS, private, or international interview is only days or weeks away, do not rush a weak book into a binder either. This page will not ask a family to assemble a layout template and call it a formal publication. Publishing plus a listing fits the next cycle that still has about a year.
