When preparing for Hong Kong international school interviews, many parents share a common myth: my child must be an "all-rounder." Consequently, portfolios are stuffed with certificates in sports, music, languages, and STEM.

But when you sit across from the interviewer, you'll find they often brush off these "all-rounder" resumes. Why?

## "All-Rounded" Often Means "Mediocre"

In the applicant pool for top international schools, being "excellent" is just the baseline. When every applicant plays the piano and swims, these skills won't make you stand out. Interviewers review dozens of similar resumes daily; their brains automatically filter out repetitive information.

What they are truly looking for are **"Spiky"** students—children who demonstrate exceptional passion and depth in one specific area.

## How to Showcase Uniqueness?

To showcase uniqueness, you need a medium that proves your child's "deep involvement." This is why more and more high-income families are choosing **child achievement publishing**.

Imagine two interview scenarios:
- **Student A**: Hands over a "Science Hobby Class Certificate of Completion."
- **Student B**: Hands over an illustrated book they wrote themselves, *The Insect Guide of My Backyard*, complete with a recommendation from a university biology professor.

Who will the interviewer be more interested in? Undoubtedly, Student B.

## The Interview Advantages of a Book

1. **Controlling the Narrative**: During the interview, the child can speak confidently about the book they wrote. This not only shows confidence but lets the interviewer see their genuine passion.
2. **Tangible Achievement**: A physical book is a concrete, visible outcome, far more persuasive than abstract grade scores.
3. **Demonstrating Soft Skills**: Completing a book requires perseverance, creativity, and problem-solving—the exact soft skills international schools value most.
