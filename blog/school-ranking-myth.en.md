Parents around me talk constantly about “first tier” and “second tier.” My daughter is at a school people call “second tier.” For a while I was very anxious.

## What an interviewer told me

I once spoke with an interview teacher. She said, “Rankings are the parents’ language, not ours. We see dozens of children a day. Certificates numb us. What we want to meet is the person — are they curious, do they think for themselves, will they say what they actually believe?”

When she finished, I thought of my daughter’s *Kitchen Science Experiments*. That book was not written for an interview. On interview day she took it out herself and talked for a long time.

## What sits outside the ranking

I understood later: the tier is the surface. Schools want families and children who will start things on their own. Every tier wants that.

The useful thing about a book is that it is hard to fake. You can enrol in ten classes and look busy. A book the child wrote, a professor followed, and an ISBN sits on, says a family actually finished something.

## For parents anxious about the ranking

Instead of chasing the tier, help a child build one thing that is theirs. Cooking, stars, collecting insects — if they are truly in it, you can help turn it into a book, and into the moment an interviewer does not forget.
