We came to Hong Kong on the Quality Migrant Admission Scheme. My husband works in Science Park. I left my job in Shenzhen and followed.

The first thing after settling in was finding a school for our daughter. I thought GTP status was a pass. Then I learned: schools still look for whether your child has something that belongs to her.

## The GTP trap: bringing the mainland timetable with you

I have seen too many GTP families fill every weekend the moment they arrive: English, debate, ice hockey. The reason is simple — that is how we stacked résumés on the mainland.

Admissions officers here are somewhat immune to “the busy child.” They have seen too many identical résumés.

I spoke with my daughter’s class teacher. One line stayed with me: “We want to see a child’s spark — not how packed their Saturday is.”

## How we changed our thinking

My daughter has always liked writing stories, and she draws little figures. I used to file that under “hobby-class stuff.” It did not count.

A friend later told me her son took the stories he wrote and, with a studio, turned them into a formally published book — ISBN, a professor who had read it, and a place on a bookstore platform.

My first reaction: is this actually good? Or is it just another way to buy a résumé?

After a few hours of talking, I changed my mind. The point is not “buying.” The point is that the child was in it the whole way. He wrote the story. The professor only helped lift the academic expression. He had a hand in the design too. The book that came out was *his*.

## Interview day

She brought the book. The officer asked, “How much of this did you write?” She could say which passages were hers and which she revised after the professor’s notes.

You cannot memorise that. She had actually done it.

She got an offer. I will not claim the book decided it alone. But it was the most distinctive thing in that room — because everyone else’s résumé, in the end, was the same kind of class.

## One line for other GTP families

You have already worked hard enough to move here. On education, you do not need to import the mainland habit of stacking certificates. What Hong Kong schools look for is whether a child has a voice and a piece of work of their own.

A book they truly took part in often says who they are more clearly than ten weekend-class certificates.
