I am a Quality Migrant Admission Scheme parent who crosses from Shenzhen into Hong Kong for work every day. My son is in Primary 4. When we moved, the worry was not housing or money. It was this: local children have been building things since they were small. What does my son catch up with?

Tuition, hobby classes, a stack of certificates — I tried all of it. On open day I saw that every parent in the queue ahead of me had done the same. When an interviewer meets yet another “all-subject awards” portfolio, their eyes barely move.

The turn came at an admissions talk. A principal said something I still remember: “We want to meet a real person, not a packaged résumé.” That night at home I talked with my son for a long time and asked what he most liked doing. He said he liked watching the birds near our building. He had a handwritten notebook.

I thought: what if that notebook became a real printed book, with his name as the author?

We did it. The book is not thick. He wrote it, drew it, took the photos. I only tidied the wording. When he took the physical copy to the interview, the principal opened his observation diary and asked a lot of questions about birds. For that half hour, my son’s eyes were bright.

Hong Kong admissions is not a contest of who has more certificates. A book the child led, with traces of their thinking still in it, tells the school: this is a child who explores on their own.
