Parents who hear that a child has “published a book” may be looking at a contest prize collection or a school anthology, or at a book the child finished and then took toward a physical bookstore platform. Both can have a book number. Either might sit on a shelf. An interviewer does not hear the same sentence. This page compares one piece inside a multi-author book with a sole-authored book and a bookstore listing path. A school portfolio is a later use: https://kidsmybook.com/admissions-portfolio .

## What is the difference between a prize anthology and a sole-authored book?

A contest collection, a school volume, or a selected-writing anthology is usually one book by many authors. The child has an essay, a drawing, or a prize piece inside it. The cover is often the organiser or the school. The contents page lists many names. If an interviewer asks whether this is the child’s book, the honest answer is “I was selected; I have one piece in it”, not “I finished this book myself”.

A sole-authored book starts from the child’s own interest. The child takes part in the writing and the revisions, and ends with a formally printed book they can open to one page. The Kidsmybook path is about 12 months of mentoring. The lead is publishing plus a physical bookstore listing path, not an ISBN on its own.

## If the anthology has an ISBN and a shelf, has the child published their own book?

No. An anthology can have an ISBN. The organiser or a publisher may also place the collected volume in a bookstore or a school library. A copy on a shelf shows that the collection was published. It does not show that this child finished a book of their own. The number still only identifies one version. Why an ISBN is not a bookstore shelf: https://kidsmybook.com/blog/isbn-is-not-bookstore-listing .
