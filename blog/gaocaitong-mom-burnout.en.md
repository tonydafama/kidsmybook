I am a mum who came to Hong Kong on the Top Talent Pass — and I was a parent close to burnout.

## The trap I fell into

My husband received Top Talent Pass approval and we moved to Hong Kong. I left my job in Shenzhen. The time that opened up, I poured into “planning” for our daughter: weekend olympiad maths, weekday piano, summer debate, winter volunteering. I turned my anxiety into her timetable.

One night, after she was asleep, I looked at the Excel schedule and suddenly did not know whether the sheet was for her — or to prove that I, as the wife who came with the Top Talent Pass, was doing well.

## A sentence from a professor I kept

Later I met a university professor. He said, “What is most particular about your daughter is not how much she has done. It is whether there is one thing she herself wants to do, without you pushing.”

He suggested we turn her love of birdwatching into a book. Not for admissions. So she could see that she can finish something.

The day *Hong Kong Migratory Bird Notes* was published, she said to me for the first time, on her own: “Mum, next time I want to write one about butterflies.”

That was when I knew I had helped her find herself again.

## For other parents on the same road

If you are also enrolling classes until you are exhausted, pause. Are you building a résumé, or building her?

A book will not solve everything. It will show you that the strongest part of a child is often the part you did not schedule.
