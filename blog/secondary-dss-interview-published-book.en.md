For secondary, DSS, and other secondary interviews, families often resubmit the primary certificate stack unchanged. Secondary interviews more often ask what the student kept doing, why they chose it, and what they changed when it was hard. A finished publication they can open is easier to answer with than another short course.

## What do secondary, DSS, and direct-subsidy interviews look for?

Each school sets its own criteria. Kidsmybook does not represent any DSS, direct-subsidy, or private secondary school, and there is no partnership list. In a portfolio, interviews usually look for continuity: one thing done over time, with a start, a process, and a revision the student can describe — not ten weekend classes with one certificate each. The slot is still short. A file that opens on awards is hard to separate in the first minute.

## Where should the publication sit?

At the front, as a finished project, not between certificates. One page can state the title, what the child did, whether a university professor reviewed it, the publication year, and which page they will talk about. Other activities come after. ## Can the book guarantee a secondary place?

No. A publishing studio is not an admissions agent and does not guarantee a DSS, direct-subsidy, private, or aided secondary place. The school decides. The book gives the interview a story that can be finished, and a formal publication someone can cite.
