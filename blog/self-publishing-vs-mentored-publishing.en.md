When Hong Kong parents say they want a child to “publish a book”, some mean a print shop and a family keepsake. Others mean a mentored path with formal publication. Both can be physical books. An admissions interview does not read them the same way.

## What is DIY self-publishing?

DIY publishing usually means the family supplies the text and pays a printer for a souvenir. It can look handsome, and it can be a sincere family record. A keepsake does not automatically include a mentor, academic review, an ISBN registration path, or a physical bookstore listing. If an adult wrote most of the pages, it is also harder to talk about the book as something the child actually did.

## What does mentor-led publishing add?

Kidsmybook runs a 12-month mentor-led publishing programme. It is not a DIY template and not print-only. The public path is consultation, dedicated classes, a publishing seminar with university professor review, parent-child layout, then print and listing. The child takes part, and the book keeps the child’s own voice. Launch, exhibition, media, and an admissions portfolio can be added when the family wants them.

## What does a school interview actually look for?

International, private, and DSS interviews see many similar certificates. A formal publication the child took part in is easier to remember than one more identical award: where the interest came from, what the child did, and how the work became publishable. That is portfolio evidence. It is not an offer of admission. Kidsmybook is a publishing studio, not an admissions agent. The school decides.

## Which path fits?

If the only goal is a private souvenir, a print shop can be enough. If the goal is a school portfolio — a real interest, a formal publication, and a story the child can tell — mentor-led publishing is the match. Chinese, English, or bilingual depends on the target school and is decided in consultation.
