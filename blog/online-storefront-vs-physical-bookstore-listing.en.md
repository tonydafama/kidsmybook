Parents who hear “listed” may be looking at a product page on an online shop, a title in an ebook-store catalog, or a copy on a shelf at Joint Publishing, Commercial Press, or Chung Hwa. All three sentences can say the book is listed. What a parent can hold is different. This page only separates those three. ## What are the three meanings of “listed”?

The first is a product page on an online shop. Amazon, HKTVmall, or a print-on-demand shop’s own site can show a page someone can order from, once a file or a small batch is uploaded. The second is an ebook-store catalog: a reader downloads the file or reads it on a screen. There is no copy to pick up from a shelf. The third is a physical bookstore shelf. After a formal print run, copies go through a supply arrangement. The people who buy for the chain decide whether to stock the title, where it sits, and whether unsold copies come back.

For a parent, the third sentence has a different weight. A book someone can open and carry, plus a bookstore buyer who took it in, means a person chose to put that title on a physical shelf. A product page and an ebook catalog show that a web address exists. If an admissions portfolio cites the work later, it cites the book someone can hold, not the address. ## Does “available” on Amazon, HKTVmall, or a POD shop mean Joint Publishing, Commercial Press, or Chung Hwa has it?

No. “Available online” means a seller or a platform accepted a file or a batch and put it on their own catalog. A shelf at Joint Publishing, Commercial Press, or Chung Hwa is another path. The book is printed first. Copies then move through distribution to the bookstore platform. The buyer decides stock-in, display, and returns. Kidsmybook is not those bookstores. The store can decline the title, stock only some branches, or return copies later. No branch is guaranteed to have a copy.

An ISBN is still only a book number. A book number, an online shop that says available, and an ebook catalog do not show that any physical bookstore has ordered the title.
