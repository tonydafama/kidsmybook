Parents preparing a primary, secondary, or international-school interview often hear “publish a book” and finish the thought with “an ISBN is enough”, or “print a copy and carry it in”. This page does not start from the portfolio. The Kidsmybook lead is publishing plus a physical bookstore listing: the child takes part, the book is formally printed, and the title and package then follow a supply path toward a Hong Kong physical bookstore listing / distribution path. An interview can cite that result. It is a second use. It is not the first sentence of the plan, and it is not a school place.

A teacher is unlikely to walk into a branch and count copies during the meeting. The path comes first. The three interview rooms share one sentence after that.

## What is the path behind publishing plus a physical bookstore listing?

The path does not end when a book number is issued. The front of it is an about-12-month mentored programme: a consultation, mentor-led making, a university professor review when it fits, parent-and-child layout, and only then a formal print run. Listing is discussed after the book is printed. Listing is a distributor supply path. Once the title reaches a Hong Kong physical bookstore listing / distribution path, the store buyer and the store manager decide whether to take it in, where to place it, how long it stays, and whether to return it. Kidsmybook is not a bookstore chain. Listing depends on the title and package. A title does not appear in every branch by itself. ## Why should an interview not rest on a book number alone?

A book number identifies a version. An interviewer who is handed a printed copy can already open it. What the physical listing path adds is a second sentence someone can check in kind: the book was not only registered, and it did not only stop as a copy printed at home. For that title and package, it entered a bookstore-platform supply arrangement. The interviewer does not need to audit a branch in the room. The honest line stops at “there is a listing path”. It does not continue into “so every shop has it”.

## How can one listing path be told in a primary, secondary, and international-school interview?

Use one book and one path. A primary interview or a door-knocking round lasts a few minutes: the child opens one page and talks about the interest, and a parent adds at most one line, that the book was formally printed and has a physical bookstore listing path. Secondary, DSS, and direct-subsidy interviews more often ask about a sustained interest. A personal learning portfolio can name the publication year and that listing line. The book does not replace school reports. An international-school interview uses the same facts in English: the book was printed, and it has a path onto Hong Kong physical bookstore shelves. The shop decides whether to stock it. None of the three rooms is an offer of a place.

In those few primary minutes, do not recite a branch list. The child says where the interest came from, what they did, and which page they changed. The parent’s listing sentence ends there. ## Can a listing path stand in for a school place?

No. Kidsmybook is a publishing studio. It is not an admissions agent, it has no school partnership, and it does not represent any primary, secondary, or international school. The school decides. A listing path also does not guarantee stock in every branch. A book that has entered a bookstore-platform path can give the conversation one sentence someone can check. It does not replace a school’s admissions criteria, and it does not replace school reports.
