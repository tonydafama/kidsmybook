## What is an ISBN, and is it copyright?

An International Standard Book Number identifies the title, the publisher, and the place of publication. Booksellers and libraries use it for ordering and stock. The system began in 1967 and was introduced in Hong Kong in 1976. From 1 January 2007 the number grew from 10 digits to 13. Hong Kong Public Libraries states that assigning an ISBN is not a statutory requirement in Hong Kong and is not related to copyright ownership. An ISBN does not create copyright, and a book without one can still be copyrighted.

## Who assigns an ISBN in Hong Kong?

Numbers for publishers operating in Hong Kong are assigned by the Books Registration Office of the Hong Kong Public Libraries, under the Leisure and Cultural Services Department. A print shop producing the book is not the official agency. A company or organisation submits the original application form plus a copy of a Business Registration Certificate valid for at least three months, or a Certificate of Registration of a Society. An individual applies in person with the original Hong Kong identity card. Publishers with an e-Cert may apply electronically. If the documents are complete and correct, the numbers are generally posted within three to four working days to the Hong Kong address on the form. Use the contact details on the Books Registration Office page. Do not treat Kidsmybook as that office.

## Does the Books Registration Office charge for an ISBN?

The office states that no application fee is required. How many numbers are issued depends on the publisher’s actual need and past registration record. In general, each application issues no fewer than ten ISBNs. Another batch is available after about 70 percent have been used and the related books have been delivered for registration under the Books Registration Ordinance. “No application fee” describes the Books Registration Office. ## Do ISBNs expire, and can a family reuse someone else’s number?

An ISBN has no expiry date. It cannot be reused and it cannot be transferred. Numbers issued by the Books Registration Office are for the applicant only. The ISBN and publisher details printed in the book must match the office’s record. A change of business name or address has to be notified in writing with the latest certificate. Printing someone else’s number on a child’s book does not mean the book “has an ISBN”.

## What if the book is printed in Hong Kong but published elsewhere?

The International ISBN Agency’s guidance is that numbers follow where the publisher operates. Numbers from the Books Registration Office are only for publishers operating in Hong Kong. They should not be assigned to books published outside Hong Kong, including in mainland China and overseas. Hong Kong’s group identifiers are 962 or 988, and current 13-digit numbers use the 978 prefix. A book published outside Hong Kong needs a number from the agency for that place of publication. Printing a few copies in Hong Kong does not by itself create a Hong Kong ISBN.

## Is ISBN the same as books registration?

No. The ISBN is an identifier. Books registration is a separate step. Hong Kong Public Libraries cites the Books Registration Ordinance (Cap. 142): within one month after a new book is published, printed, produced, or otherwise made in Hong Kong, the publisher must deliver five copies to the Books Registration Office free of charge. Registration itself is free of charge. Books not offered to the public, newspapers published at least four times a week, and publications that are only price lists, sales catalogues, trade circulars, or trade advertisements are among the categories the page lists as not required. Contravention is an offence. The page currently states a fine of $2,000 on conviction. That penalty is statutory. It is not a Kidsmybook fee. Exemptions and procedure follow the official page and the ordinance.

## Does an ISBN guarantee a school place?

No. A number makes a book easier to identify, order, and cite, so a portfolio can describe a formal publication rather than only a keepsake. The number does not prove the depth of the work, and it does not guarantee admission.
