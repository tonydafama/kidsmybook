My daughter is in Primary 3. What she likes most is insects. A cicada shell from the school garden can hold her for an afternoon. On a summer trip to Tai Mo Shan she pulled me along and took dozens of photos.

At first I treated it as a phase. Then one day she stapled her own A4 booklet. The cover said “My Insect Friends.” Every bug inside she drew and named herself. Only then did I see how much she had noticed: which ones come out in the morning, which ones like the damp.

I talked with my husband about how to keep that heat from disappearing once the collecting stopped. A friend described children’s publishing — take what the child already made, lay it out properly, print it, and turn it into a book with an ISBN that can sit on a desk.

The process is not mysterious. We scanned her drawings. She recorded herself telling each insect’s story. Then we cleaned up the words. During layout the editor asked how she wanted the book arranged. She said the butterfly she loved most should go on the cover. In that moment I saw she was not “doing homework.” She was making a book of her own.

After it came out she took it to school constantly. Even the class teacher borrowed it for the reading corner. The surprise was that she now looks things up on her own, because “the book might need a second edition.”

An interest that becomes something you can hold feels real to a child. She knows her work was taken seriously.

If you want to try making a book with your child, Kidsmybook is a Hong Kong studio for children’s achievement publishing. They walk with you from the interest to the printed book.
