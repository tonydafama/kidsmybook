I am a parent from the mainland. “Bookstore,” to me, used to mean shopping online. Then my daughter’s insect book was printed, and the studio said we could try a Hong Kong physical bookstore listing / distribution path.

I will be honest: I did not expect much at first. You can sell online just the same. The day we actually walked into a Hong Kong physical bookstore, my daughter pulled my finger toward the shelf: “Mum — that’s my book.” She stood on her toes and touched the cover. An iPad never gives you that look.

I stood there thinking: “on the shelf” means, to a child, that your work is treated as real. Not a pile of scrap in a drawer at home. The same as other authors: sitting there for someone to walk past and pick up.

At another bookstore I watched an older woman turn a few pages and tell her granddaughter, “This girl is about your age.” My daughter heard it and stood a little straighter. Being recognised by a stranger felt more solid than a hundred compliments from me.

For parents like me there is another layer. Hong Kong admissions cares about a child’s public presence — work that was not only practised behind a closed door, but put out where people can see it. A book actually on a Hong Kong physical bookstore shelf tells a school the effort left a trail.

So if you are thinking about this, do not only think online. The copy you can hold is what a child remembers for life.

Kidsmybook is a Hong Kong children's publishing studio. A listing is a bookstore-platform path for a title and a package. It does not mean every copy appears in every shop.
