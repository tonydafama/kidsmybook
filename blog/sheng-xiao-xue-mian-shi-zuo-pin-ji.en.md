Hong Kong parents searching for a primary portfolio are usually in one of three moments: a discretionary-place interview, door-knocking after central allocation, or a transfer. All three are short. A principal may not read every certificate page.

## How much should a primary interview portfolio include?

Less, clearly told, usually works better than a thick folder. The common problem is too many copies of speech, art, swimming, and piano certificates, while the child cannot say what they actually did. The interview is looking for one sustained interest and whether the child can describe it in their own words.

A book can be the object for that interest. Length should match primary age: observation, drawings, short sentences, and one revised page, rather than a long essay written by a parent. Kindergarten depth is covered separately: https://kidsmybook.com/blog/you-zhi-yuan-zuo-pin-ji-chu-shu .

## How do you use a book in a few interview minutes?

Choose one page the child explains: where the interest came from, what they did, and what they changed. A parent adds the process. Do not hand the child a script. A formal publication is something they can hold, not one more identical certificate.

### On the day

- Before the interview, pick one page: the start of the interest, one observation, or one revision.
- The child opens that page and speaks.
- If the teacher follows up, talk about what was hard and what changed.

## Does publishing guarantee a primary place?

No. Kidsmybook is a publishing studio, not an admissions agent, and does not guarantee a place at any primary, DSS, or international school. The school decides. A book can give the portfolio a story someone remembers. It does not replace the school's criteria.

The named public case is Hilary: age 8 at publication, a bilingual butterfly guide from her own observation, then a launch and a media record the family could put in an admissions file. That is a publishing example, not an offer. Case: https://kidsmybook.com/case-studies/hilary-butterfly-guide .
