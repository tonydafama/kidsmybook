I work in education, so I notice this: Hong Kong schools care a great deal about how a third party sees your child.

A mother praising her own son — who believes that? A certificate from a tuition centre was bought. But if a real university professor is willing to write a few lines for a book a child wrote, the weight is different.

After my son’s bird book came out, the studio made an introduction. A biology professor at CUHK read it. He did not brush it off. He wrote a long email about how careful the observation methods were, and suggested adding a distribution map. In the end he agreed to a short piece for the back cover.

On the day of the secondary interview I hesitated about bringing the book. The principal picked it up himself, saw the professor’s words, and looked up: “How do you know this professor?” My son said plainly that the observations were his, and the studio had made the contact. The principal nodded and did not ask about other certificates.

What I learned: a professor’s involvement is not a back door. It is proof that the book and the child can stand a professional eye. He said something a parent cannot say and a certificate cannot say — this child’s exploring was serious.

Not every book finds a professor. If the content is real, the chance tends to come. For me, the process itself was already the best lesson my son had.
