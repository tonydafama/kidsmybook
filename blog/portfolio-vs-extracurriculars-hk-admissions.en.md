Everyone takes extracurricular classes. Why doesn't the admissions officer remember your child?

Parents in Hong Kong are incredibly diligent. From kindergarten, piano, violin, swimming, and olympiad math are almost "standard equipment." But when you attend an elite school interview, you discover a harsh truth: **thousands of children's portfolios contain exactly the same certificates.**

## The Limitations of Traditional Extracurriculars

Traditional classes and grading exams have their value, but as admissions portfolio highlights, they face two major problems:
1. **Severe Homogenization**: When 80% of applicants have Grade 8 piano, it's no longer a "highlight"—it's a baseline.
2. **Lack of Personal Voice**: A certificate proves a child "studied" or "passed," but it reveals nothing about their personality, passion, or way of thinking.

## Physical Book Publishing: A Portfolio Game-Changer

In contrast, transforming a child's genuine interest into a **formally published, ISBN-registered physical book** has a completely different impact during an interview.

No two children will write the exact same book. Whether it's *My Insect Observation Diary* or *Science Experiments in the Kitchen*, a book represents the child's exclusive perspective. When the interviewer turns the pages, they see a living, breathing person, not a cold certificate.

### 3. Long-term Dedication
Completing a book takes months. This proves to the school that the child possesses high levels of focus, patience, and execution—the exact traits top schools look for.
