We came to Hong Kong on the Quality Migrant Admission Scheme. My husband does R&D in Science Park. I left my Shenzhen job and followed. The first thing on the ground was finding a school place for our daughter — every parent here is competing for one.

It took me half a year to see this:

## Everyone’s résumé looks the same

Olympiad maths, violin, swimming, community service. On interview day I sat in the waiting area and heard the family ahead say almost exactly what I had prepared. Teachers see dozens of children a day. By the third, their eyes have gone still.

My daughter likes watching the stars. On ordinary nights she pulls me onto the balcony for meteors and draws her own star maps. I used to think that was useless — not a “talent.” Then an admissions consultant asked, “Does she have one thing she loves that you have always treated as useless?”

I said stargazing. She said, “That may be the most valuable part.”

## How a book makes the story concrete

The consultant told me some families turn a child’s interest into a formally published book with an ISBN. Not a print run to give away. Mentors and university professors stay with the work: start from the child’s observations, and shape a book she actually helped write.

Her book, *Balcony Astronomy Notes*, uses star maps she drew and observations she wrote. A professor helped straighten the structure. On interview day she held it and talked about a meteor shower for fifteen minutes. The teacher asked, “She wrote this herself?” She nodded. That moment beat ten certificates.

## Why this matters for GTP families

Coming from the mainland, we often equate “Hong Kong education” with “spend more and enrol more.” What schools here want to see is whether a child is clear about who they are. A book that starts from their interest and carries a professor’s name is the easiest way to show that.

They do not need to be a genius. My daughter is in Primary 5. A mentor tightened the sentences. The story is hers. Nobody can stand in for that.

## If you are thinking about this too

If you are a GTP family and the admissions anxiety is sitting with you, ask: does your child have one thing they truly like — something you have filed as “useless”?

That thing may be the part of the résumé that actually stands out.
