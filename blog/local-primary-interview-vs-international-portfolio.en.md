The same child-made book can be prepared for a local primary and an international school. A separate article covers two ways of telling one story inside the interview room, not two stories: https://kidsmybook.com/blog/international-vs-local-school-interview-portfolio . This note does not repeat that. It only covers submission: when the file goes in, and where the book sits.

## When is a local primary file handed in, compared with an international-school portfolio?

A common pattern: at a local primary discretionary interview or door-knocking visit, the portfolio is opened that day. The time is short, and a principal may not read every page. An international school often asks for a portfolio or supporting papers at application, then follows up on one item in the interview. Schools differ. This is not any school's official rule. Kidsmybook has no school partnership and is not an admissions agent.

## Where should the same book sit in the file?

In a local primary file for that day, put the book first and certificates or tutoring drafts behind it. Plan to open one page. Door-knocking files: https://kidsmybook.com/blog/primary-school-door-knocking-portfolio-prep . Primary use: https://kidsmybook.com/blog/primary-interview-how-to-use-a-book . In an international-school application portfolio, the book is one finished project: where the interest came from and what the child did. The interview is where they explain it aloud. Why one deep project is easier to remember than a sample of everything: https://kidsmybook.com/blog/international-school-interview-portfolio-highlight .

## Can you submit two stories that contradict each other?

No. The application form, the portfolio, and the day-of file should be one interest and one book. Language can be Chinese, English, or bilingual for the school, decided in consultation. Two files that do not match are the easiest thing to catch in a follow-up question.
