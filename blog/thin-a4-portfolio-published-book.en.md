Parents who see “portfolio: two to five A4 pages”, or “no portfolio, certificates only”, often finish the thought with “fill the pages in Canva or another layout template”, or “photocopy the whole book into the binder”. This page does not start from a page-count template. The Kidsmybook lead is publishing plus a physical bookstore listing: the child takes part, an about-12-month mentored programme prints the book, and the title and package then follow a supply path toward bookstore platforms such as Joint Publishing, Commercial Press, or Chung Hwa. A school portfolio can cite that result. It is a second use. It is not the first sentence of the plan, and it is not a school place.

When DSS and private schools accept a portfolio, families often hear a limit of two to five A4 pages. Some schools state that they accept no portfolio, or certificates only. The page count, whether a portfolio is accepted, and whether a physical object may be carried in, follow that school’s latest guidelines. The publishing path comes first. What to put inside a page limit comes after that. The task is not to force the whole book into the binder.

## How does publishing plus a bookstore listing relate to a page limit?

The book is a finished publication first. The portfolio only decides how many pages may cite it. The path does not end when a book number is issued. The front of it is an about-12-month mentored programme: a consultation, mentor-led making, a university professor review when it fits, parent-and-child layout, and only then a formal print run. Listing is discussed after the book is printed. Listing is a distributor supply path. Once the title reaches platforms such as Joint Publishing, Commercial Press, or Chung Hwa, the store buyer and the store manager decide whether to take it in, where to place it, how long it stays, and whether to return it. Kidsmybook is not those bookstores. Listing depends on the title and package. A title does not appear in every branch by itself. ## If the school allows only two to five A4 pages, should the whole book be photocopied into the binder?

No. Two to five pages is the file the school asked for. It is not an invitation to take the book apart and photocopy it until the sleeve is full. A full photocopy usually breaks the page limit, and what the interviewer sees is a copy, not a formal publication. One A4 page is enough: the title, the child’s part, the ISBN, and an optional line on the physical bookstore listing path. The remaining pages stay for whatever else the school asked for. The hub: https://kidsmybook.com/admissions-portfolio . The checklist: https://kidsmybook.com/admissions-portfolio-checklist .

That one page can say the following, still inside the school’s latest form:

- The book title
- What the child did: where the interest came from, the child’s part, and which page they changed
- The ISBN, which identifies this version
- An optional line: formally printed, with a physical bookstore listing path for this title and package; the store decides stock, display, and returns

Do not put a branch guarantee, an offer of a place, or a price on that page. The only named public case is Hilary, who was eight and spent about a year on a butterfly guide. ## Are bringing the physical book on interview day and uploading a PDF portfolio the same thing?

No. Uploading a PDF hands over a file. The school reads it in its own system. Bringing the physical book means that, on the day, there is a publication someone can open, and the child points to one page. The two can happen in sequence. They do not replace each other. If the school wants a PDF, submit that one-page note in the page count and upload format it asked for. Do not scan the whole book past the limit. If the day allows an object, bring the book, point to one page while speaking, and do not read from the cover to the last page.

## If the school accepts no portfolio, or only certificates, should a family still submit a thick binder?

No. If the school states that it accepts no portfolio, or certificates only, do not submit a thick binder, and do not photocopy the book in behind the certificates. Submit certificates as the school asked. Keep the physical book for the interview day. If the day allows an object, bring it, and speak from one page: the title, what the child did, and optionally one line on the listing path. If the day does not allow the book, say that same page aloud. Do not add a file the school did not ask for.

## Can a published book guarantee a school place?

No. Kidsmybook is a publishing studio. It is not an admissions agent, it has no school partnership, and it does not represent any primary, secondary, or international school. The school decides. A listing path also does not guarantee stock in every branch. Page limits, whether a portfolio is accepted, and whether a book may be carried in, follow that school’s latest guidelines. A book can give the conversation one publication someone can check. It does not replace a school’s admissions criteria, and it does not replace school reports or certificates.
