I cross the border from Shenzhen every day for work. Last year, while applying to Hong Kong international schools for my son, I noticed something: everyone was holding the same résumé — olympiad maths, swimming, strings. By the third child, the interviewer’s eyes had already gone still.

One sentence from that interviewer I still remember: “We want to see what belongs to the child — not the classes the parents signed them up for.”

Why does that matter?

## A résumé is a story, not a pile of certificates

International schools are not looking for “the cleverest child.” They are looking for the child who is clearest about who they are. My son likes insects. On ordinary days he picks up fallen leaves and watches ants move house. I used to think that was a waste of time — until a teacher asked him, “What do you most like to study?” He talked about ants for ten minutes.

The teacher’s reaction: this child has observation, has patience, and will stay with something.

A maths certificate cannot say that. A book he helped write, adapted from his own life, can.

## How one book becomes an admissions highlight

I later learned that some families turn a child’s interest into a properly published book with an ISBN. Not a vanity print run to give away — mentors and university professors stay with the work:

- Month 1: talk with the child first, and find out what they actually like (not what the parent thinks is useful)
- The months after: a professional mentor and a university professor guide the research, drafting, and design
- At the end: a real print run that can sit on a bookstore platform

My son’s book, *Notes on Watching Ants Move House*, he held and talked through in the interview. The teacher asked, “He wrote this himself?” He nodded. That moment beat ten certificates.

## Why this matters especially for mainland parents

We often treat “Hong Kong education” as “spend more and enrol in more classes.” What international schools here actually look for is whether a child has their own voice. A book that starts from their interest and carries a professor’s endorsement is the easiest way for someone to see who that child is.

They do not need to be a literary genius. My son is in Primary 4. The sentences were tightened by a mentor. The story is his. Nobody can substitute that.

## If you are thinking about this too

If you also cross the border every day and feel the admissions anxiety, ask yourself: does your child have one thing they truly like — something you have always filed as “useless”?

That thing may be the most valuable part of their résumé.
